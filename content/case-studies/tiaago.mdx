## Context and problem

Recruiting teams spend most of their time on work that scales poorly: reading resumes, assessing skills and finding candidates beyond their existing pipeline. The result is slow shortlists and missed talent.

The opportunity was to automate the high-volume parts of hiring with AI, expand sourcing reach far beyond a single database, and let clients run structured hiring inside their own products instead of only inside ours.

## Who it's for

Recruiters and hiring teams at B2B clients, and the HR and EdTech platforms that wanted hiring capability inside their own products. Their job to be done is to move from an open role to a qualified shortlist faster, with confidence in why each candidate was surfaced.

## Discovery and key insight

Partnerships across HR and EdTech communities gave direct access to how recruiting teams and platforms actually work, and a channel for early adoption.

**The insight that shaped the roadmap:** recruiters did not need more candidates, they needed fewer, better ones sooner. Value sits in screening and sourcing quality and in how well the product fits existing workflows. That pointed to automated screening plus very large-scale sourcing, delivered in a form clients could embed.

## Options considered and decisions

| Decision | The alternative | What I chose | Trade-off accepted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What to automate first | Broad suite of features | Resume screening and AI skill assessment first | Targets the largest block of recruiter time and the clearest time-to-shortlist metric |
| How to source | Build a talent database from scratch | Integrate an 850M-profile dataset | Reach at scale immediately, in exchange for data pipeline and quality design work |
| Product shape | Closed application only | Pipeline as a Service | Clients build on Tiaago as infrastructure, which raises the bar for API reliability and documentation |
| Pricing | Single flat subscription | Flexible subscription plus usage-based models | Revenue tracks client value and is more predictable, but pricing has to be designed with cost-to-serve in mind |

## AI approach

I partnered with engineering on ML model integration, data pipeline architecture and API development, so the AI features were technically sound and scalable rather than demo-grade. The AI-facing capabilities were automated resume screening and AI-driven skill assessment, both aimed at shortening the route to a shortlist. Sourcing depended on data pipeline design that made an 850M-profile dataset usable inside the product.

## What I'd do differently

I would define a quality measure for AI screening decisions, such as agreement with recruiter judgment, before scaling the sourcing dataset, so volume never ran ahead of trust. I would also start the Pipeline as a Service conversation with clients earlier, because it turned a feature set into a platform and was the part clients valued most.
