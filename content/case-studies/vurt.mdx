## Context and problem

Cross-border payments in and out of African markets suffer from settlement delays, uneven reliability and fraud exposure. Users lose trust quickly when a transfer is slow or unclear, and the business loses money when fraud passes through weak identity checks.

## My role and ownership

| Area | What I owned |
| --- | --- |
| Settlement and FX | Led feature development for cross-border settlement and multi-currency exchange systems |
| Payments performance | Integrated real-time transaction processing APIs to reduce settlement delays and improve reliability |
| Risk | Strengthened KYC and fraud detection workflows |
| Research and analytics | Conducted user research and synthesised data analytics to find friction points and shape iterations |

## Who it's for

Individuals and businesses who need to send, receive and exchange money across currencies and borders, and the operations and risk teams responsible for keeping those flows safe.

## Discovery and key insight

I ran user research and analysed product data to locate friction in the transaction journey. These findings informed product iterations that improved usability and retention.

**The key insight:** settlement speed and fraud control are one problem, not two. Users judge reliability by how quickly and predictably money lands, while the business judges it by how little fraud gets through. Improving one at the expense of the other would not hold.

## Options considered and decisions

| Decision | The alternative | What I chose | Trade-off accepted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Settlement speed | Batch-style processing | Real-time transaction processing APIs | Higher integration effort in exchange for fewer delays and a more reliable user experience |
| Fraud control | A separate fraud layer added on top | Reworked KYC and fraud detection as one flow | Identity quality and transaction monitoring reinforce each other, at the cost of touching onboarding itself |
| FX | Bolt FX onto settlement later | Design multi-currency exchange and settlement together | A larger first build, but consistent transactions across markets |

## AI approach

This was a fintech and risk product, not an AI-led one. The technical leverage came from API integration for real-time processing and from detection workflows that flagged suspicious transactions during rollout. I worked at the boundary of product, engineering and risk to decide where each check belonged in the flow.

## What I built

- **Cross-border settlement and multi-currency exchange** across African and global markets.
- **Real-time transaction processing APIs** integrated into the payment flow.
- **Strengthened KYC and fraud detection workflows.**
- **Research-driven iterations** based on user research and analytics.

## How I ran delivery

1. **Diagnose:** user research and analytics to find where users stalled or lost trust.
2. **Prioritise:** rank friction by impact on settlement reliability and fraud exposure.
3. **Ship:** release settlement, FX and KYC improvements in iterations.
4. **Measure:** track fraud and settlement outcomes through rollout.

## Outcomes

| Metric | Result | What drove it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transaction-related fraud | **Down 30% within 6 months of rollout** | Strengthened KYC and fraud detection workflows |
| Settlement delays | **Significantly reduced** | Real-time transaction processing APIs |
| Payment reliability | **Improved for end users** | Faster, more predictable settlement |
| Usability and retention | **Improved** | Iterations informed by user research and analytics |

## What I'd do differently

I would set a fraud-versus-friction budget at the outset, so every added check carried an explicit conversion cost the team had agreed to in advance. I would also measure settlement time by corridor from day one, because averages across markets hide the corridors that most need attention.
