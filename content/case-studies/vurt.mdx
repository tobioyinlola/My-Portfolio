Vurt is a fintech product for moving money across African and global markets. I led feature development across cross-border settlement and multi-currency exchange, and strengthened the KYC and fraud controls around them.

## Context and problem

Cross-border payments in and out of African markets suffer from settlement delays, poor reliability and fraud exposure. Users lose trust quickly when a transfer is slow or unclear, and the business loses money when fraud passes through weak identity checks.

## Who it's for

Individuals and businesses who need to send, receive and exchange money across currencies and borders, and the operations and risk teams responsible for keeping those flows safe.

## Discovery and key insight

I ran user research and analysed product data to find the friction points in the transaction journey. These findings directly informed iterations that improved usability and retention.

The key insight: settlement speed and fraud control are one problem, not two. Users judge reliability by how quickly and predictably money lands, while the business judges it by how little fraud gets through. Improving only one at the expense of the other would not hold.

## Options considered and decisions

- **Real-time transaction processing APIs.** I integrated real-time processing to cut settlement delays and improve payment reliability for end users.
- **Strengthen KYC and detection together.** Rather than adding a separate fraud layer, I reworked KYC and fraud detection workflows as one flow, so identity quality and transaction monitoring reinforced each other.
- **Multi-currency exchange as a core capability.** Seamless transactions across African and global markets required FX and settlement to be designed together from the start.

## AI approach

This was a fintech and risk product, not an AI-led one. The technical leverage came from API integration for real-time processing and from detection workflows that identified suspicious transactions during rollout. I worked at the boundary of product, engineering and risk to decide where checks belonged in the flow.

## What I built

- **Cross-border settlement and multi-currency exchange** features across African and global markets.
- **Real-time transaction processing APIs** integrated into the payment flow.
- **Strengthened KYC and fraud detection workflows.**
- **Research-driven iterations** based on user research and analytics.

## Outcomes

- Achieved a **30% reduction in transaction-related fraud within six months** of rollout.
- Significantly reduced settlement delays and improved payment reliability for end users.
- Delivered usability and retention improvements traced to research and data analysis.

## What I'd do differently

I would set a fraud-versus-friction budget at the outset, so every added check had an explicit cost in conversion that the team had agreed to in advance. I would also measure settlement time by corridor from day one, because averages across markets hide exactly the corridors that most need attention.
